<h2>Getting Our Dogs Into Panama</h2>
<p>The paperwork for the dogs was another thing that had us really stressed. If we didn't get this right, we'd find ourselves in a situation where we could come into the country but the dogs would be sent back to the States. No way we could let that happen; we'd have to go back, too. Non-advance purchase airfare would have been very expensive, and when we got back to the States, we'd be homeless. The whole scenario would have cost us a fortune. </p>
<p>The conventional wisdom (as found on various Panama Expat Facebook groups and other outdated sources) was that you had to get the International Health Certificate from a USDA certified veterinarian (that part is true), <em><strong>which is only good for 10 days </strong></em>(also true), Fedex it (next day) to the official Panamanian office to be apostiled (certified) along with the required fee — <em>in cash</em> — and a prepaid Fedex return envelope so that you could get it back within the 10 days (and, of course, before you get on the airplane.) So many people talk about how stressful this is. </p>
<p>The vet where we went to get our babies their international health certificate had told us to go to aphis.usda.gov — "<a href="https://www.aphis.usda.gov/es/print/pdf/node/3677">This is the bible</a>", she told us, for traveling with pets outside the U.S. On that page, I noticed the following statements:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>"If you live in a state that does not have a Panamanian diplomatic office in it, the health certificate can be signed (apostilled/ legalized) by the Office of Authentications, Bureau of Consular Affairs (U.S. Department of State) located in Washington, D.C."</em></p>
</blockquote>
<p>and...</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>"Note: Effective 12/31/2020, Panama has temporarily waived the apostille requirement. The requirement for post-APHIS endorsement stamp and signature by a Panamanian diplomatic office/consulate still remains a condition of travel."</em></p>
</blockquote>
<p>When I sent in the initial paperwork (application for home quarantine), giving them advance notice that we were coming to Panama with two dogs, I mentioned those statements and asked for confirmation. They responded that (since Panama does not have a Embassy/Consulate in Nevada) we were good to go. All we needed to do was show up with the International Health Certificate, vaccination records, and the required fees. It felt like we really dodged a bullet; what a load off our minds!</p>
